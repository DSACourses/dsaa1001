    Course Details

    DSAA1001 · Fall 2026 · Module 1

    Module Overview

    DSAA1001 introduces beginners to the lifecycle of data science problems and solutions. Module 1 develops the mathematical and algorithmic foundations needed to model uncertainty, summarize large data streams, estimate unknown quantities, test claims, and make decisions under uncertainty.

    Assessments

    The Module 1 assessment is worth 20% and consists of:

    1. Four in-class tests (16%)
    2. Answers to the questions on the learning sheets, submitted through the AI platform (4%)

    There will be four 30-minute closed-book tests at the beginning of Lectures 3, 5, 7, and 9.

    • They are closed-book tests; an instructor-provided cheat sheet and an ordinary calculator are allowed.
    • Each test covers the knowledge and skills from the preceding two lectures. For example, the test at the beginning of Lecture 5 covers Lectures 3 and 4.
    • Validation sheets will be provided to help you prepare. Test sheets will use similar question types and formats, but the specific content and level of depth may vary.
    • The average score across the four tests determines the 16% test component of the Module 1 assessment; the learning-sheet answers make up the remaining 4%.

    On the AI platform, students are expected to submit their answers to the "mandatory" questions on the learning sheets.

    • The questions are intended to help you learn more deeply and develop your critical-thinking skills. Perfect answers are not expected; thoughtful engagement is what matters.

    Learning Outcomes

    By the end of Module 1, students should be able to:

    1. formulate probability models and distinguish outcomes, events, random variables, values, and distributions;
    2. work with discrete and continuous distributions, conditioning, Bayes' theorem, expectation, variance, and limit theorems;
    3. explain and analyze compact streaming methods, including reservoir sampling, Morris counting, Bloom filters, and Count-Min Sketch;
    4. derive and compare maximum-likelihood, MAP, and Bayesian estimates;
    5. interpret estimator quality, hypothesis tests, p-values, predictions, and loss-based decisions correctly.

    Learning Method

    Each learning sheet uses three levels. Tier 1 gives the operational ideas, Tier 2 states the formal definitions and results, and Tier 3 develops modeling judgment. Students should prioritize Tiers 1 and 2, work actively on the embedded questions, and ask for support when a difficulty persists. Tier 3 is for deep understanding and advanced problem-solving and is not required for the test.

    AI Platform and Academic Integrity

    The course-specific AI platform is required for learning activities. Use it to clarify concepts, request hints, generate additional examples, and debug code; do not use it to replace your own thinking. During class, use the course platform rather than external AI tools. AI tools are not permitted during the Module 1 tests. When a submission requires it, declare any AI assistance and make sure you understand and can explain the submitted work.

    All students must follow HKUST(GZ)'s Academic Honor Code and the University's academic-integrity regulations.
